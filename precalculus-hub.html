📐 Pre-Calculus Content Standards

Functions

Analysis: Analyze functions using different representations (algebraic, graphical, numerical, verbal).

Building: Build new functions from existing functions (transformations, compositions, inverses).

Types: Polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, piecewise.

Trigonometry

Unit Circle: Extend the domain of trig functions using the unit circle.

Modeling: Model periodic phenomena with trigonometric functions.

Identities: Prove and apply trigonometric identities (Pythagorean, sum/difference, double/half angle).

Inverse Trig: Understand inverse trig functions and their domains/ranges.

Laws: Apply Law of Sines and Law of Cosines to non-right triangles.

Polar & Parametric

Polar: Convert between rectangular and polar coordinates; graph polar equations.

Complex: Represent complex numbers in polar form; DeMoivre's Theorem.

Parametric: Graph parametric equations; eliminate the parameter.

Vectors

Operations: Add, subtract, and scale vectors; find magnitude and direction.

Dot Product: Calculate and interpret dot product; find angle between vectors.

Applications: Work, force, and motion problems.

Sequences & Series

Arithmetic: Find nth term and sum of arithmetic sequences.

Geometric: Find nth term and sum (finite and infinite) of geometric sequences.

Notation: Use sigma notation; expand and evaluate sums.

Introduction to Calculus Concepts

Limits: Understand the concept of limit; evaluate limits graphically and algebraically.

Continuity: Understand continuity and identify discontinuities.

Rates: Average vs. instantaneous rate of change (intuitive).

👩‍🏫 Advanced Trigonometry

Beyond Right Triangles

Trigonometric Identities
  • Pythagorean: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ
  • Sum/Difference: sin(A±B) = sinA cosB ± cosA sinB
  • Double Angle: sin(2θ) = 2sinθcosθ, cos(2θ) = cos²θ - sin²θ
  • Half Angle: sin(θ/2) = ±√[(1-cosθ)/2]
Laws for Non-Right Triangles

Law of Sines: a/sinA = b/sinB = c/sinC

Law of Cosines: c² = a² + b² - 2ab·cosC

Use Law of Sines when you know AAS, ASA, or SSA (ambiguous case)

Use Law of Cosines when you know SAS or SSS

🔄 Polar Coordinates & Complex Numbers

Alternative Coordinate Systems

Polar Coordinates (r, θ)

r = distance from origin; θ = angle from positive x-axis

Conversions:

  • x = r·cosθ, y = r·sinθ (polar to rectangular)
  • r = √(x² + y²), θ = arctan(y/x) (rectangular to polar)
Complex Numbers in Polar Form

z = r(cosθ + i·sinθ) = r·cis(θ) = re^(iθ)

DeMoivre's Theorem: [r·cis(θ)]^n = r^n·cis(nθ)

Used to find powers and roots of complex numbers

➡️ Vectors

Quantities with Direction

Vector Representations
  • Component form: ⟨a, b⟩
  • Unit vector form: ai + bj
  • Magnitude: |v| = √(a² + b²)
  • Direction: θ = arctan(b/a)
Dot Product

u · v = |u||v|cosθ = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂

If u · v = 0, vectors are perpendicular

Applications: Work = F · d (force times displacement)

📈 Sequences, Series & Limits

Patterns and Their Sums

Sequences
  • Arithmetic: aₙ = a₁ + (n-1)d; common difference d
  • Geometric: aₙ = a₁ · r^(n-1); common ratio r
Series (Sums)
  • Arithmetic: Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ)/2
  • Geometric (finite): Sₙ = a₁(1 - rⁿ)/(1 - r)
  • Geometric (infinite, |r| < 1): S = a₁/(1 - r)
Introduction to Limits

lim(x→a) f(x) = L means f(x) approaches L as x approaches a

Foundation for calculus: derivatives and integrals are defined using limits

📚 Lesson 1: Verifying Trigonometric Identities

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will use fundamental identities to verify equations
  • Students will develop strategies for identity proofs
  • Students will recognize equivalent expressions

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): Review fundamental identities. Key strategy: Work on ONE side only, transform to match the other side.

Guided Practice (18 min): Verify: tanθ + cotθ = secθcscθ. Work left side: sinθ/cosθ + cosθ/sinθ = (sin²θ + cos²θ)/(sinθcosθ) = 1/(sinθcosθ) = secθcscθ ✓

Independent Practice (20 min): Verify identities with varying complexity.

Closure (5 min): "What strategies are most helpful when verifying identities?"

📚 Lesson 2: Law of Sines and Law of Cosines

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will apply Law of Sines to solve triangles
  • Students will apply Law of Cosines to solve triangles
  • Students will determine which law to use based on given information

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): "We know SOH-CAH-TOA for right triangles. What about other triangles?" Introduce both laws.

Guided Practice (18 min): Decision tree: AAS/ASA → Law of Sines. SAS/SSS → Law of Cosines. SSA → Law of Sines but check for ambiguous case.

Independent Practice (20 min): Solve various triangles; real-world applications.

Closure (5 min): "When would you choose Law of Cosines over Law of Sines?"

📚 Lesson 3: Introduction to Polar Coordinates

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will plot points in polar coordinates
  • Students will convert between polar and rectangular
  • Students will graph basic polar equations

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): In rectangular (x, y), we measure right/up. In polar (r, θ), we measure distance and angle. Both describe the same points!

Guided Practice (18 min): Plot (3, π/4), (-2, π/2). Convert (3, 4) to polar. Convert (5, π/6) to rectangular.

Independent Practice (20 min): Convert points; graph simple polar equations like r = 3, θ = π/4, r = 2cosθ.

Closure (5 min): "What does r = 3 look like in polar coordinates?"

📚 Lesson 4: Vector Operations and Dot Product

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will perform vector addition and scalar multiplication
  • Students will calculate dot product
  • Students will find angle between vectors

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): Vectors have magnitude AND direction. Review component form and operations: u + v = ⟨u₁+v₁, u₂+v₂⟩, ku = ⟨ku₁, ku₂⟩.

Guided Practice (18 min): Introduce dot product: u · v = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂. Alternative: u · v = |u||v|cosθ. Solve for angle between vectors.

Independent Practice (20 min): Vector operations; dot products; angles; orthogonality.

Closure (5 min): "How can you tell if two vectors are perpendicular?"

📚 Lesson 5: Introduction to Limits

⏱️ 50 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will understand the concept of a limit
  • Students will evaluate limits graphically and numerically
  • Students will evaluate simple limits algebraically

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (10 min): "What value does f(x) approach as x approaches 2?" Even if f(2) doesn't exist, the limit might! Use graph and table to illustrate.

Guided Practice (18 min): Evaluate limits from graphs. Use tables to estimate. Algebraically: direct substitution, factoring to remove discontinuity.

Independent Practice (18 min): Find limits various ways; identify when limit doesn't exist.

Closure (4 min): "What's the difference between f(2) and lim(x→2) f(x)?"

📖 Student Reading Passages

📚 The Golden Ratio: Mathematics of Beauty

The ratio (1 + √5)/2 ≈ 1.618... appears everywhere: in the spiral of a nautilus shell, the arrangement of seeds in a sunflower, and the proportions of the Parthenon.

This "golden ratio" φ is intimately connected to the Fibonacci sequence - the ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers approaches φ as the sequence continues. It's also the only number where φ = 1 + 1/φ!

📚 From Finite to Infinite: The Birth of Calculus

For centuries, mathematicians struggled with infinity. How can you add infinitely many numbers and get a finite answer? The concept of limits, developed by Newton and Leibniz in the 1600s, finally made sense of this.

A limit captures what happens as we approach a value, even if we never quite reach it. This idea is the foundation of calculus, which revolutionized science and engineering.

📖 Math Vocabulary

Identity

Definition: An equation true for all values in the domain

"sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is true for all θ."

Polar Coordinates

Definition: System using distance r and angle θ from origin

"(3, π/4) means 3 units at 45° from positive x-axis."

Dot Product

Definition: Operation on vectors: u·v = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂ = |u||v|cosθ

"⟨3,4⟩·⟨2,1⟩ = 6 + 4 = 10"

Convergent Series

Definition: Infinite series with a finite sum

"1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... = 2 (converges)"

Limit

Definition: Value a function approaches as input approaches some value

"lim(x→2) (x² - 4)/(x - 2) = 4"

Ambiguous Case

Definition: SSA situation where 0, 1, or 2 triangles are possible

"Given two sides and non-included angle, check all possibilities."

❓ Misconceptions

❌ sin(A + B) = sinA + sinB

Use sum formula: sin(A + B) = sinA cosB + cosA sinB

❌ Limit equals function value

Limit is what function APPROACHES, not necessarily the function value at that point.

⚠️ Important Considerations

Mathematical Accuracy
  • Always verify AI outputs: AI can make mathematical errors, especially with multi-step problems
  • Check solutions step-by-step: Verify each step before using problems with students
  • Watch for notation errors: AI may use inconsistent or incorrect mathematical notation
  • Test with edge cases: Check if AI-generated problems work with boundary conditions
Pedagogical Considerations
  • Use AI as a starting point: Refine and adapt outputs for your specific students
  • Maintain your expertise: You understand your students better than any AI
  • Model critical evaluation: Show students how you evaluate AI-generated content
  • Preserve productive struggle: Don't let AI remove the valuable learning from challenge

🔍 Mathematical Verification Skills

Always Verify AI Solutions
  • Check boundary conditions: Does the solution work at extremes?
  • Verify graphically: Graph equations and solutions to check visually
  • Substitute back: Put answers back into original equations
  • Dimensional analysis: Do units work out correctly?
  • Reasonableness check: Does the answer make sense in context?
Common AI Errors in Pre-Calculus
  • Incorrect handling of negative exponents and radicals
  • Errors in multi-step algebraic manipulation
  • Wrong interpretation of function notation
  • Mistakes with domain and range restrictions
  • Oversimplification of word problem contexts
  • Incorrect graphical interpretations
