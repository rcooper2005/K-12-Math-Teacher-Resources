👩‍🏫 Linear Functions

The Foundation for Algebra

Understanding y = mx + b

m = slope: Rate of change; rise/run; steepness and direction

b = y-intercept: Where line crosses y-axis; starting value

Key Connections
  • Slope = constant rate of change = constant of proportionality (when b=0)
  • Proportional: y = mx (through origin)
  • Non-proportional: y = mx + b, b ≠ 0
Multiple Representations

Students should move fluently between: table → graph → equation → context

📐 Pythagorean Theorem

a² + b² = c²

Understanding the Theorem

In a RIGHT triangle, the sum of squares of the two legs equals the square of the hypotenuse.

Only works for RIGHT triangles!

Applications
  • Find unknown side length
  • Distance formula: d = √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)²]
  • Determine if triangle is right, acute, or obtuse
  • Real-world: ladders, diagonal measurements, etc.

🔄 Transformations

Rigid Motions and Dilations

Rigid Motions (Congruence)
  • Translation: Slide (same size, same orientation)
  • Reflection: Flip over a line (mirror image)
  • Rotation: Turn around a point

Rigid motions preserve size and shape → congruent figures

Dilation (Similarity)

Enlargement or reduction by scale factor k

k > 1: enlargement; 0 < k < 1: reduction

Dilations preserve shape but not size → similar figures

🔢 Systems of Equations

Solving Two Equations Simultaneously

Methods
  • Graphing: Solution is intersection point
  • Substitution: Replace variable with equivalent expression
  • Elimination: Add/subtract equations to eliminate variable
Types of Solutions
  • One solution: Lines intersect (different slopes)
  • No solution: Parallel lines (same slope, different intercept)
  • Infinite solutions: Same line (equivalent equations)

📚 Lesson 1: Understanding Slope

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will calculate slope from two points using rise/run
  • Students will interpret slope as rate of change
  • Students will identify positive, negative, zero, and undefined slopes

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): Show staircase image. "How steep are these stairs?" Introduce slope as rise/run. m = (y₂-y₁)/(x₂-x₁).

Guided Practice (18 min): Calculate slope from graphs and from two points. Connect to real-world rates: "The pool fills at 30 gallons per minute" → slope = 30.

Independent Practice (20 min): Find slopes from various representations. Identify slope types.

Closure (5 min): "What does a negative slope mean in context?"

📚 Lesson 2: The Pythagorean Theorem

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will state and apply the Pythagorean Theorem
  • Students will solve for unknown sides in right triangles
  • Students will verify the theorem using area models

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): Draw right triangle with squares on each side. "The areas of the two smaller squares add up to the area of the largest square!" Demonstrate with 3-4-5 triangle: 9 + 16 = 25.

Guided Practice (18 min): Practice finding missing sides. Given a=6, b=8, find c. Given a=5, c=13, find b. Emphasize: c is ALWAYS the hypotenuse (longest side, across from right angle).

Independent Practice (20 min): Mixed practice finding unknown sides.

Closure (5 min): "A ladder reaches 12 feet up a wall. It's placed 5 feet from the wall. How long is the ladder?"

📚 Lesson 3: Transformations on the Coordinate Plane

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will perform translations, reflections, and rotations
  • Students will describe transformations using coordinate notation
  • Students will identify transformation from coordinates

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): Review three types. Translation: (x, y) → (x+a, y+b). Reflection over y-axis: (x, y) → (-x, y). Rotation 90° clockwise: (x, y) → (y, -x).

Guided Practice (18 min): Transform triangles on coordinate plane. Verify with tracing paper. Write transformation rules.

Independent Practice (20 min): Perform transformations; identify transformations from before/after coordinates.

Closure (5 min): "Point A(3, -2) is reflected over the x-axis. What are the new coordinates?"

📚 Lesson 4: Solving Systems by Graphing

⏱️ 55 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will solve systems of equations by graphing
  • Students will interpret the intersection point
  • Students will recognize parallel and coincident lines

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (12 min): "Two cell phone plans: A charges $20 + $0.05/text. B charges $30 + $0.02/text. When are they the same cost?" Write equations: y = 0.05x + 20, y = 0.02x + 30.

Guided Practice (18 min): Graph both lines. Find intersection: approximately (333, 36.67). At about 333 texts, both cost about $37. Verify by substitution.

Independent Practice (20 min): Solve systems by graphing. Include examples with no solution and infinite solutions.

Closure (5 min): "What does it mean if two lines are parallel when solving a system?"

📚 Lesson 5: Irrational Numbers

⏱️ 50 minutes

Learning Objectives

  • Students will distinguish rational from irrational numbers
  • Students will approximate irrational numbers
  • Students will locate irrational numbers on a number line

Lesson Procedure

Introduction (10 min): "What is √2? Let's see... 1² = 1 and 2² = 4, so √2 is between 1 and 2. 1.4² = 1.96, 1.5² = 2.25, so √2 is between 1.4 and 1.5." Show decimal goes on forever without repeating!

Guided Practice (15 min): Define: rationals = fractions; irrationals = decimals that never end and never repeat. Examples: √3, π, √5 are irrational. √4 = 2 is rational!

Independent Practice (20 min): Classify numbers; approximate square roots; locate on number line.

Closure (5 min): "Is √9 rational or irrational? What about √10?"

🔬 Investigation 1: Proving the Pythagorean Theorem

Focus: Geometric proof | Duration: 50 minutes

Procedure

  1. Cut out four identical right triangles
  2. Arrange to form a large square with a square "hole" in the middle
  3. Calculate area two ways: (a+b)² and c² + 4(½ab)
  4. Set equal and simplify to get a² + b² = c²
  5. Discussion: Why does this prove the theorem?

🔬 Investigation 2: Transformation Exploration

Focus: Properties of transformations | Duration: 45 minutes

Procedure

  1. Draw a triangle on coordinate grid
  2. Perform each transformation: translate, reflect, rotate 90°, 180°, 270°
  3. For each, record: coordinates, orientation, size
  4. Which properties are preserved by each transformation?
  5. Combine transformations - does order matter?

🔬 Investigation 3: Scatter Plot Analysis

Focus: Bivariate data and linear models | Duration: 2 class periods

Procedure

  1. Collect bivariate data (height vs. arm span, study time vs. grade, etc.)
  2. Create scatter plot; describe pattern
  3. Draw line of best fit
  4. Write equation of line
  5. Use equation to make predictions
  6. Discuss reliability of predictions

📚 The Pythagorean Theorem: Ancient Wisdom

Over 2,500 years ago, the Greek mathematician Pythagoras discovered something amazing about right triangles. If you draw a square on each side of a right triangle, the areas of the two smaller squares always add up to the area of the largest square!

Written as an equation: a² + b² = c², where c is the longest side (hypotenuse). This simple formula lets us find distances we can't measure directly - like the diagonal of a room or the straight-line distance between two points on a map.

📚 What Makes a Function?

A function is like a machine: put something in, get exactly one thing out. If you put in 3 and sometimes get 6 but sometimes get 9, that's NOT a function!

The "Vertical Line Test" checks if a graph is a function: if any vertical line crosses the graph more than once, it's not a function. Why? That would mean one input gives multiple outputs.

Functions are everywhere: your age is a function of the year you were born. The cost of gas is a function of how many gallons you buy.

📖 Math Vocabulary

Slope

Definition: Rate of change; rise over run; m in y = mx + b

"A line through (0,1) and (2,5) has slope = 4/2 = 2."

Y-intercept

Definition: Where a line crosses the y-axis; b in y = mx + b

"In y = 3x - 2, the y-intercept is -2."

Hypotenuse

Definition: Longest side of a right triangle (across from right angle)

"In a² + b² = c², c is the hypotenuse."

Irrational Number

Definition: A number that cannot be written as a fraction

"√2, π, and √3 are irrational - decimals never end or repeat."

Function

Definition: A rule that assigns exactly one output to each input

"y = 2x + 1 is a function; each x gives one y."

System of Equations

Definition: Two or more equations considered together

"y = 2x + 1 and y = -x + 7 form a system; solution is (2, 5)."

Translation

Definition: A transformation that slides a figure without rotating or flipping

"(x, y) → (x+3, y-2) translates right 3, down 2."

Dilation

Definition: A transformation that enlarges or shrinks by a scale factor

"Scale factor 2 doubles all lengths."

❓ Common Misconceptions

❌ Slope = rise + run (adding instead of dividing)

Error: Rise of 4 and run of 2 gives slope of 6

Correction: Slope = rise/run = 4/2 = 2. It's a ratio!

❌ Using Pythagorean Theorem on non-right triangles

Error: Applying a² + b² = c² to any triangle

Correction: ONLY works for RIGHT triangles! Check for 90° angle first.

❌ All square roots are irrational

Error: Thinking √16 is irrational

Correction: √16 = 4, which is rational. Only non-perfect square roots are irrational.

⚠️ Important Considerations

Mathematical Accuracy
  • Always verify AI outputs: AI can make mathematical errors, especially with multi-step problems
  • Check solutions step-by-step: Verify each step before using problems with students
  • Watch for notation errors: AI may use inconsistent or incorrect mathematical notation
  • Test with edge cases: Check if AI-generated problems work with boundary conditions
Pedagogical Considerations
  • Use AI as a starting point: Refine and adapt outputs for your specific students
  • Maintain your expertise: You understand your students better than any AI
  • Model critical evaluation: Show students how you evaluate AI-generated content
  • Preserve productive struggle: Don't let AI remove the valuable learning from challenge

🧒 AI for Students

Developing responsible AI use skills for 8th Grade math

📋 Age & Usage Guidelines

Important Requirements
  • Most AI tools require users to be 13+ - Check your birthdate eligibility
  • Middle schoolers may use AI only with teacher supervision and clear guidelines
  • AI use is a privilege: Misuse may result in losing access
  • Always follow your school and district AI policies
  • When in doubt, ask your teacher first

✅ Appropriate Uses (with Teacher Permission)

AFTER You've Tried the Problem First
  • Check your completed work: Compare your solution process with AI's approach
  • Get alternative explanations: "Explain Linear Functions, Pythagorean Theorem, Transformations a different way"
  • Understand word problems: Help interpreting what a problem is asking (not solving it)
  • Explore "what if" scenarios: "What would the graph look like if I changed this value?"
  • Generate practice problems: Ask for more problems like your homework to practice
  • Clarify vocabulary: "What does 'coefficient' mean in this context?"
🚫 NOT Appropriate Uses
  • Getting answers without trying: This is cheating AND means you don't learn
  • Copying solutions for homework: Your teacher will know, and you won't learn
  • Using during tests or quizzes: This is academic dishonesty
  • Skipping steps you're supposed to show: The work IS the learning

🔍 Critical Evaluation Skills for Math AI

How to Check if AI is Right
  • Substitute back: Put your answer back into the original equation
  • Estimate first: Does the AI's answer make sense based on your estimate?
  • Check each step: AI often makes errors in the middle of solutions
  • Graph it: Use Desmos to visually verify algebraic solutions
  • Try a different tool: Compare answers from Wolfram Alpha, Photomath, etc.
Common AI Math Errors to Watch For
  • Sign errors (positive/negative mistakes)
  • Distributing incorrectly
  • Misreading the original problem
  • Skipping steps that are actually required
  • Getting word problems completely wrong

🚫 What AI Cannot Replace

The Value of Productive Struggle
  • The learning IS in the struggle: When you're confused and work through it, your brain grows
  • Number sense and intuition: Only develops through practice with numbers
  • Problem-solving strategies: You learn these by solving problems, not watching AI solve them
  • Showing YOUR thinking: Your teacher needs to see how YOU reason
  • Preparing for tests: On tests, it's just you and your brain - build those skills now
